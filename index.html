Interactive display

These are the actual ads. Click them.

Every creative below is the file an ad server would receive: one HTML document, no external requests at runtime, system fonts only. Nothing here is a screenshot or a video — you are looking at the running creative.

Mobile gaming

Neon Star Shooter — pick a planet

970 × 250

Six planets, twenty levels and twenty bosses on each. The banner runs the game's own planet selector: tap a world and the scene, the name and the accent colour change. Six full-bleed game scenes are WebP-compressed and embedded in the file — 128 KB against the IAB 150 KB initial-load limit.

Tap a planet along the bottom.

The same campaign, four more sizes

300 × 600 · 300 × 250 · 728 × 90 · 320 × 50

Each size decides for itself how much of the mechanic fits. 300 × 600 gets a three-by-two grid with names; 300 × 250 and 320 × 480 get symbols only; 320 × 50 has no selector at all, because six symbols plus a button would put the tap targets under 24 px.

Playable ad — twenty seconds, then the store

Fullscreen

A hyper-casual runner that plays inside the ad. Tap to jump. Built four times, one per network: mraid.open(), FbPlayableAd.onCTAClick(), ExitApi.exit(), window.openAppStore(). This is the MRAID build. Everything is drawn in code — no image, font or model file ships in the bundle.

Tap anywhere to jump.

The same game, cut down to a banner

970 × 250

A playable does not need a fullscreen slot. This is the runner inside a standard leaderboard: one round, ten seconds, a distance counter, and the store card on the same layer as the start screen so neither can be left showing behind the other.

Tap or press space to jump.

Streaming

A mood, not a genre

300 × 600

Nobody sits down wanting "crime drama"; they want something calm but dark, or intense but light. The banner asks the question a genre list cannot: drag across a two-axis field and the nearest title in the catalogue surfaces, poster and all. Every poster is drawn in code, so the file carries six of them and still weighs 11 KB.

Drag anywhere inside the field.

Food delivery

The bowl fills as you build it

970 × 250

Price, calories and protein move with every tap, and the bowl has a real capacity: a base, a protein and four add-ons fill it, and the fifth is refused. An ad that let you stack twelve toppings would be selling something the kitchen does not make.

Tap ingredients on the right.

Energy

Two bills, same house

970 × 250

Switching ads usually ask how many kWh you use a month — a number nobody knows without fetching a bill. This one asks what everybody knows: the kind of home, and whether there is an electric car or a heat pump on it. Both itemised bills then redraw, line by line.

Pick a home, then add a car or a heat pump.

Insurance

Excess against premium

300 × 600

A balance beam, because that is what the choice actually is. Raising the excess tips the premium down — and the panel underneath shows the other half of the trade, what the same choice costs the year you claim. A calculator that hid that line would be selling; showing it is the reason the category can be advertised at all.

Tap an excess step.

Beauty

36 shades, sifted to three

300 × 250

The grid is a matrix, not a list: rows are undertone, columns are depth, so it explains the range before a single tap. Two answers and the shades that do not match shrink away from the ones that do — a movement that argues for the match far better than the sentence "we have your shade".

Answer both rows.

Travel

Low-fare calendar

970 × 250

The question a flight shopper actually has is which week is cheap, not what one fixed date costs. Pick an outbound and a return inside the ad and the fare updates; the cheapest week is marked, so the banner answers the question before the click.

Pick two dates.

Budget finder

970 × 250

The inverse mechanic: name the budget and the ad shows what fits. Cards that fall under the figure open, cards above it fade but hold their position — the list never reflows, so the eye stays where it was.

Drag the budget slider.

Finance

Repayment calculator

970 × 250

Two sliders, amount and term, and a monthly figure that moves with them. Regulated categories carry required wording, so the APR line and the "illustration only, not a quote" disclaimer are part of the layout rather than something bolted on at legal review.

Drag either slider.

E-commerce

Black Friday, in phases

970 × 250

Black Friday is not one discount, it is a timetable. All three steps are on the banner and you can price any of them. The clock is a fixed campaign timestamp rather than a relative timer, and it locks at zero instead of going negative when a campaign is extended.

Choose a phase on the right.

Free-shipping box builder

300 × 600

The one mechanic that reliably lifts basket size, run inside the ad instead of on the cart page. Add and remove items; the line to £60 fills and the message changes when the threshold is crossed.

Tap a row to add or remove it.

Size and stock

300 × 250

In fashion the shopper's first question is not the price, it is whether their size is there. Sold-out sizes stay visible with a line through them: a shopper who cannot find their size closes the tab, while a struck-through size says it sold.

Pick a size.

Scratch card

970 × 250

The discount is earned rather than announced. The covering is a canvas the pointer erases, and the code underneath is only readable once enough of it is gone — a few seconds of contact with the brand instead of a number the eye skips.

Drag across the card to scratch it.
